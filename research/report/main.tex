\begin{abstract}
We study finite-horizon approximation of a discrete dynamical system with a
fuzzy initial state on the unit interval or square. A complete partition of
the initial domain supplies transported representatives, whole-cell membership
bounds and directed trajectory enclosures. These quantities give a Hausdorff
bound between full endographs, including their common zero baseline. We state
the assumptions and prove both directed estimates, explain the spatial
recurrence and conditional fixed-horizon convergence, and exhibit an exact
counterexample to monotone improvement under splitting. Policy scores are
separated from the numerical certificate: a learned ranker may select a cell
but cannot certify its children. Exact scalar cut propagation and an attributed
piecewise-linear transport specialization provide qualified comparisons.
The empirical protocol separates 64 training, 24 validation and 120 test base
problems, retains five neural model seeds and 20 random controls, and specifies
a paired clustered analysis before final outcomes. All 3,700 eligible saved
numerical runs passed canonical replay. The selected ridge policy certified
67 of 120 test bases versus 78 for maximum-bound selection: an equal-regime
difference of $-9.17$ percentage points, with grouped 95\% percentile interval
$[-14.17,-4.17]$. Conditional median work and time ratios were 1.110 on 67
jointly certified pairs and 1.389 on seven of twelve predefined timing pairs.
The scalar audit found no verified violation in 1,200 qualified brackets
from six scalar bases;
72 upper-only exceedances remain inconclusive. These observations do not
support an advantage from this frozen learned ranker. Findings are imported
from saved, hashed evaluation and analysis artifacts. The
mathematical arguments are internally derived specializations, with an
independent-pass internal AI review; external mathematical review and the
closest full-text literature comparison remain open. No scientific novelty,
universal speedup, production suitability or joint two-dimensional reference
validation is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, contribution and claim boundaries}
Zadeh's extension principle transports fuzzy membership through a deterministic
map. Its numerical representation becomes difficult when trajectories stretch,
fold or merge. Disconnected cuts and low-height components are meaningful;
replacing them by a convex hull or normalizing every state would change the
problem. Our question is narrower than discovering a new extension principle:
can an auditable implementation adapt an initial-domain cover, bound the
resulting approximation, and evaluate refinement policies under one frozen
protocol?

The software contribution is the combination of a complete-cover numerical
recipe, immutable incumbent and last snapshots, exact scalar reference
geometry, exported learned ranking policies, evidence replay, and a local
Java/React workbench. A certificate is an assumption-bound mathematical upper
bound. A verified implementation test is evidence on the tested cases.
A measured comparison is a finite-cohort observation. These statements have
different logical roles and are not interchangeable.

Approximation, decomposition, endograph metrics and maximum-based reconstruction
are prior subjects~\cite{zadeh,roman,decomposition,decomposition2008,kupka2016,pwl}.
Publisher previews already establish substantial relevant prior art. The
complete 2007 and 2016 closest papers were not obtained through the recorded
lawful public access routes. Their detailed assumptions, constants and
equivalence to this recipe have not been resolved. The propositions below
organize elementary covering and Lipschitz arguments for this representation;
they are not presented as independently established novel theorems.

A public article transcription of the 2008 work describes a levelwise
Hausdorff metric, Cartesian fuzzy-interval inputs and local tangent
approximations. Its mathematical OCR is damaged: printed-formula and constant
verification remain open. This is not a reproduced comparison baseline.

\section{Mathematical object and representation}
Let $X=[0,1]^d$, $d\in\{1,2\}$, with the infinity norm. Let
$f:X\to X$ be continuous, and let $\mu_0:X\to[0,1]$ be upper semicontinuous.
For a nonnegative integer $t$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extension}
 \mu_t(y)=\sup\{\mu_0(x):f^t(x)=y\},\qquad\sup\varnothing=0.
\end{equation}
Compactness of a nonempty fiber and upper semicontinuity attain its maximum.
Composition of the suprema agrees with repeated extension. Positive cuts are
compact continuous images, so the transported membership remains upper
semicontinuous. Nonnormal states and an identically zero state are allowed.

The full endograph and product metric are
\begin{align}
 \E(u)&=\{(x,a)\in X\times[0,1]:0\leq a\leq u(x)\},\\
 d_{\Box}((x,a),(y,b))&=\max\{\norm{x-y},|a-b|\}.
\end{align}
We write $\dE(u,v)$ for the Hausdorff distance of these compact endographs.
Every endograph includes $X\times\{0\}$, not merely transported zero-height
representatives. The distance is at most one. Coordinates and grades are
dimensionless; rescaling either changes the metric. Membership is not
probability, and grades are neither summed nor renormalized.

Let $\mathcal P$ be a finite compact cover of $X$. Boundary overlap is harmless.
Each cell $C$ has $x_C\in C$, stored $a_C\in[0,1]$, and numerical representatives
$y_{C,t}\in X$. Assume, for every $x\in C$ and $0\leq t\leq T$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:hypotheses}
 |a_C-\mu_0(x_C)|&\leq\sigma_C,&
 |\mu_0(x)-\mu_0(x_C)|&\leq\kappa_C,\\
 \mu_0(x)&\leq M_C\leq1,&
 \norm{f^t(x)-y_{C,t}}&\leq\rho_{C,t}.
\end{align}
These are whole-cell premises, not conclusions of sampling. The finite state is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite}
 \widehat\mu_t(y)=\max\bigl(\{a_C:y_{C,t}=y,\ C\in\mathcal P\}\cup\{0\}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Colliding representatives use maximum. Missing a height-one peak is permitted;
its effect must be captured by the bound.

\section{The certificate and its proof}
\subsection{P1: two directed distances}
Put $v_C=\kappa_C+\sigma_C$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 b_{C,t}=\min\{\max(M_C,a_C),\ \max(\rho_{C,t},v_C)\},
 \qquad B_t=\max_{C\in\mathcal P}b_{C,t}.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[P1: covering certificate]
Under the preceding premises, $\dE(\mu_t,\widehat\mu_t)\leq B_t$ for every
$t\in\{0,\ldots,T\}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $(y,a)\in\E(\mu_t)$. At $a=0$ the point is shared. Otherwise choose a
fiber maximizer $x$ with $f^t(x)=y$ and $\mu_0(x)\geq a$, and choose a cell
$C$ containing $x$. The approximate endograph contains
$(y_{C,t},\min(a,a_C))$. Its spatial error is at most $\rho_{C,t}$, and
\[
 (a-a_C)_+\leq|\mu_0(x)-a_C|\leq\kappa_C+\sigma_C.
\]
Independently, $(y,0)$ is shared and gives distance $a\leq M_C$. Thus the
forward directed distance is at most
\[
 D(\E(\mu_t),\E(\widehat\mu_t))
 \leq\max_C\min\{M_C,\max(\rho_{C,t},v_C)\}.
\]
For the reverse direction, let $(y,a)\in\E(\widehat\mu_t)$ have $a>0$.
Some finite maximizing cell has $y=y_{C,t}$ and $a\leq a_C$.
The true endograph contains
$(f^t(x_C),\min(a,\mu_0(x_C)))$. Spatial error is at most $\rho_{C,t}$;
vertical error is at most $\sigma_C$. The shared baseline also gives $a_C$,
so
\[
 D(\E(\widehat\mu_t),\E(\mu_t))
 \leq\max_C\min\{a_C,\max(\rho_{C,t},\sigma_C)\}.
\]
Each cell contribution in both estimates is bounded by $b_{C,t}$.
Taking their maximum proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

The cap needs $\max(M_C,a_C)$ for a general two-sided grade error. If the true
membership is zero but a stored atom has height one, $M_C=0$ alone would give
an invalid zero cap; $\sigma_C=1$ correctly records the error. The shipped
lower-grade recipe is more restricted, but the general statement should not
silently assume it. Noninjectivity, shared boundaries and representative
collisions do not require injective matching.

\subsection{A canonical outward membership recipe}
For a verified whole-cell range $[\ell_C,u_C]$, a verified representative range
$[p_C,q_C]$, and a whole-domain membership Lipschitz bound $K$, the recipe uses
\begin{align}
 a_C&=p_C,&\sigma_C&=\up(q_C-p_C),&M_C&=u_C,\\
 \kappa_C&=\min\left\{
  \max\bigl(\up(u_C-p_C),\up(q_C-\ell_C)\bigr),
  \up(K\rho_{C,0})\right\}.
\end{align}
Here $\up$ is directed rounding toward positive infinity at a declared
significant decimal precision. Each candidate bounds the same exact variation,
so their minimum is safe. The certificate uses an upward-rounded
$\kappa_C+\sigma_C$. Initial grades are transported unchanged, so membership
variation is not multiplied at every time step. Decimal evidence is serialized
as strings; model parameters begin as exact rational text.

\subsection{P2: spatial propagation and independent tightening}
\begin{proposition}[P2: one-step enclosure]
Suppose $Q_{C,k}$ contains both $f^k(C)$ and $y_{C,k}$, and $f$ is
$L_{C,k}$-Lipschitz on this complete region. If
$\norm{y_{C,k+1}-f(y_{C,k})}\leq\eta_{C,k}$ and $\rho_{C,k}$ is valid,
then $L_{C,k}\rho_{C,k}+\eta_{C,k}$ is a valid next radius.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Insert $f(y_{C,k})$ and use the triangle inequality:
\[
 \norm{f^{k+1}(x)-y_{C,k+1}}
 \leq L_{C,k}\norm{f^k(x)-y_{C,k}}+\eta_{C,k}
 \leq L_{C,k}\rho_{C,k}+\eta_{C,k}.
\]
\end{proof}
The implementation takes the convex hull of the reachable interval box and
the representative before bounding the derivative or analytic Lipschitz
constant. A representative outside a tightened box must still be included.
For $f(x)=x^2$, the derivative at representative zero is zero, yet the cell
$[0,1]$ reaches one. This refutes a representative-only derivative rule.

The midpoint of an outward singleton image supplies the next numerical
representative; that interval's radius supplies $\eta$. Products and sums
round upward. An independently propagated interval image supplies a second
valid enclosure. Their intersection remains valid; a disjoint intersection
is an arithmetic failure. To describe residual accumulation with a global
$L$, define the \emph{exact-arithmetic comparison sequence}
$r_{C,0}=\rho_{C,0}$ and $r_{C,k+1}=Lr_{C,k}+\eta_{C,k}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residuals}
 \sup_{x\in C}\norm{f^t(x)-y_{C,t}}\leq r_{C,t}
 = L^t\rho_{C,0}
  +\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}L^{t-1-k}\eta_{C,k}.
\end{equation}
This identity concerns the exact comparison sequence, not an upper bound on
the stored outward-rounded radius $\rho_{C,t}$. The latter can exceed the
unrounded expression because of arithmetic slack. P1 uses the actual saved,
conservatively rounded radius, including that slack and any valid interval
tightening; it never substitutes the unrounded expression for a certificate.
P2 concerns spatial radii. It does not multiply the entire endograph distance
by $L$: the unchanged membership coordinate gives a product-map factor
$\max(1,L)$. Even a constant spatial map need not erase grade differences.

\section{Refinement, convergence and finite resources}
\subsection{P3: validity is independent of the ranking policy}
Every committed split replaces one leaf by both closed longest-side children.
The initial cover is the complete unit box, including zero-membership regions.
Both child computations must finish before replacing their parent. Cancellation
or a failed child discards the incomplete split and preserves a complete prior
snapshot. Exact binary leaf-prefix collapse verifies coverage without a
floating-point volume test.

P1 depends on the returned cover and quantities, not on a ranking score.
A learned, random or adversarial selector therefore cannot change certificate
validity if it preserves those premises. It can profoundly change cost and
termination. Removing an inconvenient cell is not a valid policy operation.
Every fifth adaptive selection uses FIFO fairness; deterministic ties and
child creation order are recorded.

\begin{proposition}[P3: conditional fixed-horizon convergence]
Assume finite global Lipschitz constants $L$ for $f$ and $K$ for $\mu_0$,
exact representative/grade arithmetic, unlimited refinement and fair
longest-side bisection. If $r$ is the largest initial cell radius, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convergence}
 \max_{0\leq t\leq T}\dE(\mu_t,\widehat\mu_t)
 \leq \max\{\max_{0\leq t\leq T}L^t,K\}\,r
 =\max\{1,L^T,K\}\,r,
\end{equation}
where $L^0=1$, including $L=0$. For fixed $T$, the bound tends to zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Exact P2 gives $\rho_{C,t}\leq L^t r$ and membership regularity gives
$\kappa_C\leq Kr$, with $\sigma_C=0$. Insert these into P1. Any fixed
leaf has only finitely many older leaves at its creation; periodic FIFO
selections remove them, while new children cannot precede it. Thus every
fixed leaf is eventually split. In fixed dimension, repeated longest-side
bisection halves each coordinate after a bounded number of branch splits.
For any target diameter there are only finitely many ancestral cells needing
splits, and fairness eventually processes each. Hence the largest radius
tends to zero.
\end{proof}

Upper semicontinuity alone is insufficient for this convergence statement.
For identity dynamics, membership one at an irrational point and zero
elsewhere is upper semicontinuous. Dyadic midpoints never encounter its peak:
every finite midpoint approximation is zero and remains at distance one.
This does not contradict P1, which can still return a bound of one, or P3,
whose Lipschitz membership premise fails.

The implemented solver has fixed decimal precision, maximum depth, cell and
refinement budgets, and cooperative cancellation. Nonvanishing residuals in
\eqref{eq:residuals} or grade errors can impose a floor on the attainable
certificate. P3 is neither an
arbitrary-tolerance guarantee for that finite run nor a uniform-in-time result
or an efficiency theorem. A budget exit retains a bound without claiming the
requested tolerance. Initialization failure has no complete certificate.

\subsection{Exact nonmonotone refinement counterexample}
At $t=0$, consider
\begin{equation}
 \mu_0(x)=\max\left\{
  \tfrac12\max(0,1-|x-\tfrac12|),\
  \max(0,1-20|x-\tfrac1{10}|)\right\}.
\end{equation}
The root representative is $(1/2,1/2)$, with certificate $1/2$. At the narrow
peak top $(1/10,1)$, the approximate vertical segment is at spatial distance
$2/5$ and vertical deficit $1/2$, giving distance $1/2$. The baseline is at
distance one. The witness and upper bound agree: actual error is $1/2$.

After one split, the representatives are $(1/4,3/8)$ and $(3/4,3/8)$.
At the same peak top their vertical deficits are $5/8$, and the first has
spatial distance $3/20$, so the nearest distance is $5/8$. The left cell
certificate is also $5/8$. Actual error has increased. No rounding is involved.
This internal example motivates retaining both the earliest best bound
snapshot and the last completed partition; it is not asserted to be a new
phenomenon.

\section{Qualified scalar references and attributed baselines}
\subsection{Complete positive cuts and an exact staircase bracket}
For a tent peak of height $h>0$, width $w$ and center $c$, its positive
$\alpha$-cut is empty for $\alpha>h$, and otherwise
\[
 [c-w(1-\alpha/h),c+w(1-\alpha/h)]\cap[0,1].
\]
The cut of the maximum of peaks is the complete union; gaps and singleton
cores remain. The supported continuous scalar maps send each compact interval
to an interval whose exact endpoints follow from endpoints and included
critical points. Affine maps have none; tent and logistic maps turn at $1/2$.
Rational arithmetic preserves these calculations. This is not a joint
two-dimensional geometry construction.

For exact positive cuts $A_j=[\mu_t]_{\alpha_j}$ with
$0=\alpha_0<\cdots<\alpha_m=1$, set
\[
 s_t(x)=\max(\{\alpha_j:x\in A_j\}\cup\{0\}),\qquad
 \delta=\max_j(\alpha_j-\alpha_{j-1}).
\]
Then $0\leq\mu_t-s_t\leq\delta$: the lower neighboring grid level gives a
vertical match, including the zero baseline below the first level.
Thus $\dE(\mu_t,s_t)\leq\delta$. If
$d=\dE(s_t,\widehat\mu_t)$ is computed exactly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bracket}
 \max(0,d-\delta)\leq\dE(\mu_t,\widehat\mu_t)\leq\min(1,d+\delta).
\end{equation}
The exact-cut provenance is a premise. A caller-supplied list with valid
level order is not thereby an exact cut trajectory of the declared problem.

For completeness, the continuous staircase distance is not an endpoint raster.
For a nonempty source cut union $A$ at height $h$ and target atoms $(p_i,g_i)$,
\[
 F_h(x)=\min\{h,\min_i\max(|x-p_i|,(h-g_i)_+)\}.
\]
Order atoms by decreasing grade and put $v_k=(h-g_k)_+$,
$P_k=\{p_1,\ldots,p_k\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{x\in A}F_h(x)=\min\{h,\min_k\max(v_k,D(A,P_k))\}.
\end{equation}
Every prefix covers all points at its candidate radius, proving one inequality.
Conversely, if the supremum is $R<h$, every point has an atom within spatial
distance $R$ and vertical deficit $R$. The complete prefix of atoms with
$v_i\leq R$ therefore covers $A$ at radius $R$, proving the other. This avoids
interchanging a supremum and a minimum without justification. Equal grades
use the full tied prefix. Empty atoms leave the baseline distance $h$.
Empty source cuts contribute no rectangle and are skipped in this directed
maximum; they do not acquire a positive baseline-cap contribution.

For the reverse direction each atom top has distance
\[
 \min\{g_i,\min_{j:A_j\ne\varnothing}
       \max(\dist(p_i,A_j),(g_i-\alpha_j)_+)\}.
\]
Maximize over atom tops and source rectangles. Directed interval-union
distance needs source endpoints and target-gap midpoints inside a source
component. For source $[0,1]$ and targets $\{0,1\}$, endpoints alone miss
the actual maximum $1/2$ at the center.

Only an analytic or verified bracket lower endpoint strictly above its
certificate is a violation. An upper-only exceedance is inconclusive. A sampled
grid discrepancy is not promoted to a continuous lower bound. Empty positive
cuts retain explicit both-empty, reference-empty and approximation-empty
statuses with absent ordinary Hausdorff distances. Weak components may be
invisible at a small endograph scale and remain important in these diagnostics.

\subsection{Published PWL core}
The second representation independently specializes Section 4.1 of Kupka and
Nicole Škorupová~\cite{pwl}. It splits source linear pieces at map knots,
transports each branch and reconstructs by pointwise maximum. Exact fractions
handle reversed branches, constant images, spikes and support-boundary jumps.
For the eligible affine and tent maps, a complete restricted trajectory is
exact and has error zero. An incomplete transition after a piece-budget exit
cannot claim a full-horizon zero bound.

The paper's nonlinear PSO preprocessing and published timings are not
reproduced. Logistic and coupled maps are unavailable for this specialization.
Unavailable rows must remain in the declared comparison. The alpha-grid and
PWL representations incur different exact-rational work and geometry costs;
their operation counts are not equated with CAE interval map-image calls.

\section{Learned ranking and frozen experimental design}
\subsection{Prediction is a selection heuristic}
The learning target is the signed local horizon-bound change from a complete
trial split. If $b_C=\max_t b_{C,t}$, an illustrative target notation is
\[
 G_C=b_C-\max\{b_{C_\mathrm{left}},b_{C_\mathrm{right}}\}.
\]
The frozen label recipe, rather than this notation alone, defines the actual
features and target. Signed values preserve harmful splits; a gain prediction
does not assert that refinement improves actual error. Available cell features
are recorded with a versioned order and are computed before the trial outcome.
The ranker influences selection while Java independently computes every child
and certificate. Nonfinite scores and parity failures are errors, not reasons
to accept a guessed bound.

Feature means/scales and target standardization are fitted on training data
only. Ridge regression uses regularization one. The fixed neural model has
16 tanh hidden units and a linear output, with regularization $0.01$ and
L-BFGS training. Model seeds are $11,23,37,51,71$. Exported weights and
normalization are checked against Python predictions and Java inference.
Binary floating point is allowed for heuristic scores, not for directed
certificate bounds. No final-test feature or outcome row enters fitting,
normalization, architecture choice or validation selection.

\subsection{Cohorts, comparators and resources}
The manifest separates 208 distinct base problems: 64 training, 24 validation
and 120 held-out test bases. The four test regimes contain 30 bases each:
in-distribution scalar, in-distribution two-dimensional, out-of-distribution
scalar and out-of-distribution two-dimensional. All variants of a base remain
in one split. The frozen final protocol supplies the executable definitions;
the intended settings are precision 24, tolerance $0.1$, at most 512 completed
splits, and ID horizons 2/4 versus OOD horizons 6/8.

All 32 methods remain declared: uniform, maximum-bound, next-step and
predicted-gain CAE policies; ridge; five MLP seeds; 20 random-priority seeds
0--19; exact scalar alpha grid; and the eligible PWL core. Random controls
share the certificate/fairness engine and are descriptive, not candidates for
a winning seed selected on final data. The deterministic comparator is chosen
on validation by certification count, median algorithm map images, then method
ID. The learned family is chosen on validation by certification rate and median
map images. Every MLP seed remains if that family is primary.

Only 12 bases are preselected for timing: indices 000, 010 and 020 in each
regime. They receive one excluded warmup and five timed repetitions; the other
108 receive one numerical observation whose duration is retained but excluded
from timing conclusions. All 120 bases enter certification and work analysis.
Timings include numerical solving and post-solve verification, including
in-solve inference. Reference preparation, label generation, training,
diagnostics, report I/O and cold JVM startup are excluded. External workstation
load is uncontrolled. Repetitions are repeated observations, not new problems.

\subsection{Primary estimand and resampling}
For regime $r$, base $b$, variant $v$ and retained model seed $s$, let
$I^L_{rbvs}$ and $I^D_{rbv}$ indicate certification. The primary estimand is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Delta=\frac14\sum_{r=1}^4\frac1{n_r}\sum_{b=1}^{n_r}
   \frac1{|V_b|}\sum_{v\in V_b}\frac1S\sum_{s=1}^S
       (I^L_{rbvs}-I^D_{rbv}).
\end{equation}
Positive values favor the learned family. Budget exits, cancellations,
numerical failures and unavailable primary requests are non-success; none is
silently dropped. The declared analysis uses 10,000 percentile bootstrap draws,
seed 20261117, with complete bases resampled within each regime. Model-seed
indices are sampled once per draw and shared across all bases and regimes.
This crossed structure retains the fact that one fitted model is used on many
problems. Cells, time steps, refinement records and timing repetitions are not
resampled as scientific instances. Ridge has one fit, so it supplies no estimate
of training-seed uncertainty.

Secondary learned/deterministic time and map-image ratios are conditional on
both runs certifying and positive costs. Variant ratios are geometrically
combined within a base/seed; the reported statistic is a median of those
pairs. They do not establish unconditional speedup. Replicates with no eligible
pair remain unavailable; intervals require at least 90 percent valid draws.
Only aggregate certification is primary. Per-regime intervals and controls
are descriptive, without multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory interpretation.

Completion curves retain eligible failures in the denominator and use run
median durations, not within-run first-hit times or Kaplan--Meier estimates.
Training amortization is unavailable unless a complete comparable training/
label/selection cost record is supplied and every primary timing pair certifies
with positive mean savings. A favorable successful subset cannot justify a
break-even forecast.

\section{Interpretation and limitations}
\subsection{The primary learned-efficiency hypothesis was not supported}
Ridge certified 67 of 120 bases (55.83\%), whereas maximum-bound certified
78 (65.00\%). The corresponding budget-exhausted counts are 53 and 42;
neither primary method has another failure or unavailable status. The primary
equal-regime difference is $-9.1667$ percentage points, with the frozen grouped
95\% percentile interval $[-14.1667,-4.1667]$. Every one of the eleven
discordant primary certification pairs favors maximum-bound.

The ID scalar regime has a success ceiling: both policies certify all 30
bases, so a degenerate zero bootstrap interval there does not establish
population equivalence. ID 2D accounts for seven discordant bases, OOD scalar
for three, and OOD 2D for one. OOD 2D has only one primary tolerance success in
total, leaving little resolution for conditional cost comparisons under this
budget. Its absent joint continuous reference also prevents interpreting
noncertification as a measured large true error.

Other methods remain descriptive. Uniform refinement certified 47/120 bases,
next-step 73/120 and predicted-gain 62/120. Maximum-bound's 78/120 therefore
also exceeds uniform's observed attainment under the same resource limits;
this is not a complexity theorem or an additional confirmatory test. The five
retained neural fits certified 45, 58, 49, 45 and 47 bases respectively, in
seed order 11, 23, 37, 51 and 71. Selecting seed 23 after observing that table
would change the question. All fits retained their iteration-cap warnings.
The result concerns these bounded fits and protocol, not all neural networks
or every possible learned refinement objective.

\subsection{Conditional cost and a concrete resource exit}
For the 67/120 jointly certified primary pairs, the conditional median
map-image ratio is 1.109589 with descriptive 95\% interval
$[1.000000,1.202470]$. On seven of twelve predefined timing pairs, the
conditional median total-time ratio is 1.388827 with interval
$[1.196698,1.620587]$. The other 53 work pairs and five timing pairs remain
non-successes in the certification evidence. These ratios cannot be promoted
to unconditional speedups, and uncontrolled workstation load limits timing
interpretation. There is no comparable complete pipeline-cost record for
training amortization.

As a post-hoc illustration, select the lexically first base with disagreeing
primary certification outcomes, \code{test-id-2d-000}. Maximum-bound reaches
tolerance with 317 cells, a bound approximately 0.07594013 and 2,532 algorithm
map images. Ridge first retains its best bound approximately 0.12303150 at
444 cells. Its last partition has 513 cells and the same bound after
exhausting 512 splits, with 4,100 map images overall. Both snapshots pass the
same certificate recipe. This exposes an observed cost of different
refinement orders without identifying which feature or training decision
caused it. It is an illustration selected by identifier after the results,
not an independent confirmatory endpoint or a representative sample.

\subsection{What the scalar audit establishes}
The 1,200 qualified scalar discrepancy brackets, from six bases across
180 method runs and their time points, contain no verified lower
endpoint above its certificate. The 72 upper-only exceedances are
inconclusive, including uncertainty introduced by a qualified reference;
they must not be relabeled as either proven violations or proven agreement.
The complete audit and empty-cut statuses remain in the saved analysis.

\subsection{Scope of inference}
The experiment can support conclusions about this finite declared cohort,
retained models and bounded numerical recipe. It cannot establish that learning
generally improves adaptive simulation. A degenerate empirical bootstrap
interval is not proof of zero population uncertainty. The timing cohort has
only three bases per regime, and local timing uncertainty includes factors
not represented by resampling. Raw per-seed and failure rows remain part of
the evidence even when their narrative is inconvenient.

Certified bounds may be conservative; exact scalar reference enclosures can
diagnose that conservatism without constituting a general continuous proof.
No scalar-only result validates joint two-dimensional geometry. A small
endograph distance permits a weak component to move substantially or disappear
above a diagnostic cut. Fixed precision and finite budgets can prevent a
tolerance success even where the exact-arithmetic convergence argument applies.
The qualified PWL baseline has restricted eligibility and is not evidence for
an exact general nonlinear or multivariate solver.

The verifier recomputes canonical geometry and replays the declared recipe,
but shares trusted interval primitives with generation. It is not an
independently implemented proof assistant. Independent exact/analytic tests
and a second-pass internal derivation check reduce particular risks without
removing the need for external expert review. Hashes establish consistency of
bytes and provenance links, not authorship authentication, trustworthy timing
or mathematical truth.
